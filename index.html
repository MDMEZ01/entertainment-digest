📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Hollywood Quietly Killed the Blank-Check Overall Deal

  • Why It Matters
    • Peak TV's era of guaranteed eight- and nine-figure overalls is largely over — only proven multi-hit creators like Shonda Rhimes, Taylor Sheridan, Ryan Murphy, Dan Fogelman, Greg Berlanti, Chuck Lorre and Bill Lawrence can still command top dollar, while guaranteed upfront money elsewhere is being replaced by success-only bonuses and "kickers."
    • The repricing isn't just happening at the top: mid-tier writers are increasingly chasing so-called "showverall" deals tied to delivering one specific hit — Dungeon Crawler Carl co-showrunner Eric Heisserer's new NBCU pact is an early template — meaning the model is being rebuilt from the middle out, not just trimmed at the top.
    • Flexibility itself has become a bargaining chip: Seth MacFarlane reportedly requested trading his exclusive Fuzzy Door overall for a smaller-money NBCU first-look deal specifically to regain the freedom to sell projects elsewhere — a direct reversal of Peak TV's "exclusivity above all" logic.
  • The Money
    • Netflix's new five-year Rhimes deal is described as one of the last remaining nine-figure exclusive overalls in the marketplace, even as comparable Peak TV-era deals — Steve Levitan at Disney, Ava DuVernay at Warner Bros. TV — have expired without renewal at similar value.
    • J.J. Abrams' Bad Robot saw its Warner Bros. TV overall downgraded because, per one studio exec, "wasn't getting a return" — signaling that even top-tier pedigree no longer guarantees blank-check money.
  • Historical Context
    • Echoes the original overall-deal boom itself: agents note the model emerged because Apple TV+, Amazon and Netflix "had to pay people up front to steal them" from networks and legacy studios — the same inflationary logic that's now unwinding as those same streamers tighten spending.
  • Watch For
    • Whether more Peak TV-era exclusive deals quietly lapse without renewal heading into awards season, thinning the ranks of guaranteed overalls further.
    • Whether "showverall" deals like Heisserer's become a template other studios copy to lock down one-hit creators without a multi-year guarantee.
