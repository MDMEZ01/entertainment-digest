Today's Deep Dive

A Machine-Checked Riemann Proof: Anthropic's Claude and Math's Biggest Prize

Why it matters
  • Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge released a Lean proof of the 1859 Riemann Hypothesis on Nov. 20, saying he developed it with Claude.
  • On Nov. 21 the Lean and Mathlib communities, backed by the Lean FRO, said they have "full confidence" the 17-million-line proof is correct.
  • Fields medalist James Maynard: "If this fully checks out, it is clearly a historic milestone in mathematics."
The money
  • The Riemann Hypothesis is one of the Clay Institute's Millennium Prize problems, each carrying a $1M award.
  • The claim lands the day after Anthropic's public debut, as its shares tested the $83 IPO price.
Historical context
  • Appel and Haken's 1976 computer-assisted Four Color Theorem proof faced years of skepticism because no human could check every case.
Watch for
  • Mathematicians, including Maynard and Youness Lamzouri, say understanding why the proof works could take months or years.
  • Formal verification is not journal peer review; watch for a readable write-up and the Clay Institute's response.
