📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Zaslav's Plan B: The Downside Math If Ellison's $110 Billion WBD Bid Collapses

Why It Matters
  • Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín's decision to lock in the March 2, 2027 trial date and reject a fast-track hearing guarantees eight more months of daily ticking fees — roughly $7 million a day starting Oct. 1 — turning time itself into the deal's biggest cost.
  • LightShed's Rich Greenfield argues Larry Ellison's team must settle with CA AG Rob Bonta on the legal merits alone, dismissing Ellison's and CLO Makan Delrahim's "weaponization of antitrust" framing — echoed by JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon — as unlikely to sway a courtroom.
  • If the deal dies, Ellison stands to lose roughly $11 billion combined in Netflix and WBD breakup fees plus advisor costs for a "subscale," US-only Paramount worth an estimated $10B market cap — while Zaslav has a credible fallback waiting.
The Money
  • Zaslav's Plan B: pocket the $7 billion breakup fee if no deal closes by June 4, 2027, using it to cut WBD's net debt from $30B to roughly $23B and drop leverage from 3.4x to 2.6x EBITDA — enough to reach investment grade — while keeping the Streaming & Studios unit intact.
  • WBD's Q2 results show why that unit is worth protecting: Streaming & Studios revenue topped $3B (+10% YoY) with EBITDA up 60% to $512M, even as total company revenue fell 11% to $8.7B (missing the $9.2B estimate) on what Wolfe Research's Peter Supino called "studio flops in 2Q."
Historical Context
  • Greenfield points to Disney's sale of 50% of A&E Networks to Hearst at a depressed 3.5x EBITDA multiple as the going rate for forced divestitures — a preview of what WBD's cable networks (TNT, TBS, CNN) or even IP like Harry Potter and DC Comics could fetch if Bonta demands a structural remedy.
Watch For
  • Whether Ellison's team shifts from Delrahim's public "weaponization" posture toward one of Greenfield's proposed remedies — divesting a studio with its theatrical arm and major IP, or spinning off the cable troika — as advisors quietly float a CNN/CBS editorial oversight board to defuse the separate political-control objection (see Breaking News).
  • Whether continued public pressure from Dimon and Delrahim on state regulators softens Bonta's posture before pretrial deadlines, and whether Lionsgate's Jon Feltheimer-style public cheerleading for the deal spreads to other self-interested dealmakers.
