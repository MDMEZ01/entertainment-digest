Entertainment Strategy Guy

Why Netflix and YouTube Fighting Over Talent Proves Neither Has "Won" the Streaming Wars

Why It Matters
  • Netflix spent heavily in 2025–26 poaching podcasters and YouTube stars for its TV lineup, then YouTube fought back hard to keep its own top creators — a defensive move that undercuts the idea YouTube's flywheel is self-sustaining.
  • If winners had to fight this hard for talent, the analyst argues, they haven't actually won: real winners don't chase talent, talent comes to them.
  • The piece frames the conflict as entertainment's own "Thirty Years' War" — nominally two sides (tech vs. legacy media) but really a shifting multiparty scramble, unlikely to resolve cleanly for years.
The Money
  • Focus Features has taken the inverse approach with "Obsession," using YouTube as a scouting ground for new IP rather than bidding for proven stars — a cheaper, slower-burn strategy other studios are watching.
  • The column separately flags growing skepticism about whether a federal film tax credit will actually pass, and questions about what could pull Apple out of the streaming business altogether.
Historical Context
  • The analyst invokes "The Curse of the Media Mogul" — a recurring pattern where executives who declare victory in a media transition are usually the ones about to be proven wrong by the next one.
Watch For
  • Whether more traditional studios copy Netflix's direct-poaching playbook versus Focus Features' scouting model in the coming months.
  • Any signal on the stalled federal film tax credit, and whether AMC's new distribution push or Apple's streaming struggles accelerate further consolidation.
