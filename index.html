📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Karen Bass Joining the Push for a Paramount-WBD Settlement Signals the Endgame Is Near

Why It Matters
  • Bass, who previously opposed the merger outright unless job losses were addressed, now publicly urges a "quick settlement" — the first sitting elected LA official to actively push both sides toward the table rather than just criticize the deal. New Today
  • Her intervention lands one day after AG Bonta's own tonal shift ("coming to the table is always on the table"), meaning two of the deal's fiercest institutional critics have both used dealmaking language within 24 hours of each other.
  • The underlying legal leverage hasn't moved: the 12 state AGs' antitrust theory still targets cable-network concentration, not the jobs and production-flight issues driving Bass and exhibitors — so a political "settlement" mood could still leave the core antitrust case unresolved.
The Money
  • LA County's study (10,000 jobs, $79M lost tax revenue over three years) gives the mayor's office a concrete municipal financial stake distinct from the state-level antitrust fight, strengthening her case for a negotiated fix.
  • Paramount's Oct. 1 relocation threat and its $1.9B bond request — with a hearing set for Sept. 24 — keep financial pressure mounting on the company regardless of the improving political mood.
Historical Context
  • Local political pressure has occasionally nudged federal antitrust fights toward negotiated resolution before — echoing how city officials have weighed in on airline hub-city job concerns even though the controlling legal theory sits at the federal or state level, not municipal.
Watch For
  • Whether Bonta's office responds publicly to Bass's comments, and whether an actual sit-down gets scheduled before the Sept. 24 bond hearing.
  • Whether SAG-AFTRA, still withholding support for Cinema United's framework, treats Bass's intervention as reason to harden or soften its own holdout.
