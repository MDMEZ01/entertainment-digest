📈 Today’s Deep Dive

UK Clears Paramount-WBD — But the U.S. Fight Just Got More Dangerous for Ellison

Why It Matters
  • The UK's Aug. 6 clearance leaves the $110B deal approved in 66 jurisdictions worldwide, but the fight that actually decides its fate — California's antitrust suit — still heads to a March 2, 2027 Oakland trial, meaning international "yeses" are becoming a sideshow to one courtroom.
  • Analyst Rich Greenfield says Ellison is running out of time before trial and the June 4, 2027 deal deadline and is publicly calling for "substantial structural remedies, including shedding CNN" — but per Puck's Dylan Byers, Ellison's team has "zero intention" of offering the state AGs anything, betting everything on an outright courtroom win.
  • The price the UK extracted for approval — a five-year ban on linear-streaming consolidation, Channel 5 protected through 2034, and editorial firewalls around CNN International, CBS News, Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network — previews exactly the kind of remedies California could demand if Ellison's position weakens.
The Money
  • Paramount is accruing merger "ticking fees" that will total at least $1.2B by the close of the roughly three-week trial, a cost that compounds regardless of the outcome.
  • A new Puck "The Town" podcast from Matt Belloni and Eriq Gardner frames the standoff around a $650 million clock, underscoring how directly time itself has become a line item in this deal.
Historical Context
  • Puck's William Cohan has floated a "Plan B" where WBD's Streaming and Studios business could instead combine with Comcast's NBCU spinoff Versant — an idea Cohan says he first discussed with CNBC co-founder Tom Rogers back in August 2022, three years before this bid even existed.
Watch For
  • California AG Rob Bonta and Paramount chief legal officer Makan Delrahim are set to appear separately at a Politico California event in Sacramento next week — a platform either side could use to signal a shift in posture.
  • Whether Judge Nunley's "shocking" rebuke of Nexstar for stacking Tegna's board becomes a precedent state AGs cite against Paramount's own post-close integration plans ahead of trial.
