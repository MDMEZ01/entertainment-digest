📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why the Paramount-WBD Endgame Is Really a Battle Over Who Blinks First on California

Why It Matters
  • The WSJ's weekend report that talks with AG Bonta are "advanced" suggests three weeks of leak-driven brinkmanship — Nashville scouting trips, a Paramount-commissioned exit-cost study, TMZ rumors — may have been working as leverage rather than a real relocation plan, exactly as Puck's Belloni argued last week.
  • Newsom's Friday signing of AB 2319 and SB 186 reads as a direct counter-move: shoring up California's production-incentive case right as the state's negotiating position is under public pressure.
  • Belloni's central point still holds: even Musk's Tesla/SpaceX and Larry Ellison's Oracle keep major California operations despite their founders' public antipathy toward the state — talent concentration is hard to replicate 2,000 miles away.
The Money
  • A $1.9B bond hearing lands Thursday, with Paramount reportedly wanting states and the WGA to help front it; the $7M-a-day ticking fee on the $110B+ deal begins around Oct. 1 if unresolved.
  • Ellison, RedBird Capital and Middle East backers are exposed to roughly $80B in debt if the WBD deal closes — a load that makes cost-cutting attractive regardless of how the relocation threat resolves.
  • Wall Street is already voting: WBD (+7% to a 52-week-high $29.74) and Paramount Skydance (+5% to $10.76) rallied Monday on settlement optimism, though analysts call it deal-specific repricing rather than a sector move (Communication Services ETF up just 0.7%).
Historical Context
  • THR has drawn a parallel to 1934, when studio chiefs threatened to flee California for Florida over a gubernatorial race — a threat that proved to be a bluff then, too.
Watch For
  • Whether a settlement lands before the Oct. 1 fee deadline — NY's James and CT's Tong are still holding out over worker protections and CNN/CBS editorial-independence guarantees; no deal sends it to a March 2, 2027 antitrust trial.
  • Thursday's bond hearing, and whether Ellison ever goes public with a relocation plan rather than leaving it to leaks.
