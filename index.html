OpenAI AI Copyright Case Goes to Summary Judgment — DOJ's 'Joan Didion' Defense

Summary judgment briefing has begun in the landmark AI copyright cases (publishers and book authors vs. OpenAI/Microsoft). The Trump DOJ submitted a brief with a narrow conception of market harm, using the analogy of a teenage Joan Didion typing out Hemingway to "learn how the sentences worked" — asking whether she would have owed him money when she later published. The Copyright Office's view is that licensing markets matter significantly. More than 120 content-AI licensing agreements have been identified; $1B+ spent on book training rights. The outcome will shape whether AI training licenses are legally mandated or commercially optional. — Puck / Eriq Gardner
