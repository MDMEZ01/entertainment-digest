📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Why a Two-Week Delay Is a Bigger Problem for David Ellison Than It Looks

Why It Matters
  • The extended TRO keeps Paramount’s $110B WBD acquisition frozen until at least Aug. 17 — and every week of limbo hands the state AGs and the WGA more runway to build their antitrust case.
  • Judge Martínez-Olguín’s willingness to extend, citing the defendants’ own agreement to abide by the freeze, signals the court sees real questions worth a full injunction hearing — not a rubber stamp.
  • For a shrinking pool of buyers, a stalled megadeal freezes the whole town’s deal calendar: producers, agents and talent are all waiting to see how big the combined buyer becomes.
The Money
  • That “ticking fee” now bites: per the deal contract, Paramount owes ~$6.9M per day if the transaction isn’t closed by Sept. 30 — turning every day of delay into a direct cash cost for David Ellison.
  • Paramount also faces a $7B breakup fee if regulators kill the deal, while WBD stock trades at a steep discount to the offer — the market pricing in real doubt it closes on time, or at all.
Historical Context
  • The last time a media merger this size drew a coordinated multistate challenge, it reshaped the timeline for years — a reminder that antitrust delay, not denial, is often the real deal-killer.
Watch For
  • Aug. 3: the scheduled hearing on the states’ motion for a preliminary injunction — the fight that decides whether the freeze becomes long-term.
  • This is the maximum TRO extension available; if the states win the injunction, the deal stays frozen until the case is decided — and every day past Sept. 30 costs Ellison ~$6.9M, pressuring him to settle rather than litigate.
