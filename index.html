📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Meta's $1.4 Trillion Trial Is Really a Referendum on Whether Big Tech Can Self-Regulate

Why It Matters
  • 29 state AGs are testing whether internal Meta documents and COPPA-era protections can support one of the largest consumer-protection judgments ever sought — a realistic ask near $200B against a theoretical $1.4T ceiling.
  • Whistleblower Arturo Béjar's testimony about internal harm-reporting data — which Meta fought to keep out — could become the evidentiary template plaintiffs use against every platform, not just Meta.
  • The trial lands the same week Anthropic's Dario Amodei and ex-Trump AI czar David Sacks publicly clashed over whether Washington needs "a DMV for AI" — Meta's case is shaping up as the live test of what happens when no such body exists and states improvise enforcement instead.
The Money
  • Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers will decide the case herself with only an advisory jury — a structure that could move faster than a typical trial, with testimony expected to run at least six weeks.
  • Meta's defense leans on its since-added age-verification and parental-control tools, arguing product design reflects user demand and is protected speech — a strategy other platforms are watching closely as their own liability exposure grows.
Historical Context
  • The case echoes 1990s state-AG tobacco litigation, where internal company documents proved more damaging than any regulation — a playbook AGs are explicitly reapplying to social media.
Watch For
  • Whether attorneys general call Zuckerberg, Adam Mosseri or Antigone Davis to testify — a decision they said Tuesday they hadn't yet made.
  • Whether Judge Gonzalez Rogers limits further Béjar internal-data testimony, which could set the evidentiary bar for future state suits against TikTok, Snap and YouTube.
