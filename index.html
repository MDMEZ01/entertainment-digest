📈 Today's Deep Dive

The Matrix Goes Home: Copyright Termination Comes for Skydance

Why it matters
  • Lana and Lilly Wachowski have formally notified Warner Bros. they are terminating their 'Matrix' grant, which could cost the studio U.S. control as soon as February 2029.
  • It lands with 'Matrix 5' in development under Drew Goddard, and Puck hears the sisters have recently increased their involvement.
  • Skydance faces a stack of similar notices, from 'The Shawshank Redemption' and 'Beavis and Butt-Head' to 'ER,' which could give Sherri Crichton leverage over 'The Pitt.'
The money
  • David Ellison's company paid about $111B to fold in Warner Bros., and its franchise library is the asset these terminations chip away at.
  • Paramount's amicus brief, led by Lisa Blatt, warned authors would 'seek extortionate prices from studios.'
Historical context
  • Annapurna bought 'Terminator' rights at Cannes in 2011 knowing U.S. rights reverted to James Cameron in 2019, and Skydance later had to deal with Cameron; Ellison's team also quietly renegotiated 'The Godfather' after Mario Puzo's estate terminated.
Watch for
  • BMG v. Vetter: the Fifth Circuit allowed worldwide reclaiming, and the Supreme Court affirmed by default after four justices recused.
  • Whether Warners argues 'The Matrix' was work-for-hire under the early-1990s three-picture deal, and how Michael Mann's 'Heat' case (effective March 2029) is decided.
