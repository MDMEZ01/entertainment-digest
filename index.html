California's Production Rescue: A $1B Credit and a New Mayor May Not Be Enough

Why it matters
  • On-location filming in L.A. is nearly 29% below its five-year average, and TV production is down 53%.
  • Motion picture and sound recording jobs have fallen from 150,000 to fewer than 100,000, per The Ankler.
  • THR's Steven Zeitchik argues the credit 'completely ignores the biggest disadvantage California has.'
The money
  • Outgoing Gov. Gavin Newsom proposes lifting the film tax credit cap from $750M to $1B a year.
  • Mayor-elect Nithya Raman promises a film czar and cheaper permits, but the Coastal Commission, the county and Sacramento's credits sit beyond her control.
Historical context
  • Newsom already more than doubled the program to $750M in July 2025, yet production kept leaving the state.
Watch for
  • Raman takes office Dec. 14 and Newsom leaves in January, so the $1B plan falls to his successor and the Legislature.
  • Whether the federal film incentive debate at AFM gives California a national backstop or a new competitor.
