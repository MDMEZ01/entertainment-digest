Puck

ABC's Kimmel Budget Squeeze Is Really a Referendum on Late Night's Future

Why It Matters
  • Disney is demanding roughly 20% in budget cuts from Jimmy Kimmel Live! just as Kimmel seeks to re-up beyond his May 2027 deal — a test of whether traditional five-night late night can survive the same economics that killed Colbert's Late Show.
  • The negotiation is inseparable from politics: Disney is actively suing FCC chair Brendan Carr over an early broadcast-license review that insiders believe is retaliation for Kimmel's Trump jokes, after Trump himself suggested broadcast licenses "be terminated" over late night's anti-Trump lean.
The Money
  • JKL! costs roughly what CBS spent on Colbert's show (~$100M/year) before cancellation last fall; combined ABC/CBS/NBC late-night ad revenue fell from $439M in 2018 to $221M in 2024 and has kept declining since.
  • Kimmel's reported $15M annual fee and options like cutting a costly Brooklyn taping week are on the table, alongside folding in his Emmy-winning Who Wants to Be a Millionaire hosting gig as part of a broader macro deal.
Historical Context
  • CBS's cancellation of Colbert's Late Show last fall — timed just before David Ellison's Skydance closed its $8B Paramount acquisition — set the precedent that "budget" rationales for axing politically inconvenient late-night hosts get accepted largely at face value, even when the underlying economics are disputed.
Watch For
  • Whether OConnell and Kimmel's camp land on a deal blending cuts with expanded duties, or Kimmel walks; prediction market Kalshi currently prices a 9% chance he's "fired" by year-end.
  • Whether Disney's active FCC lawsuit signals it will hold firm this time, unlike last fall's chaotic, swiftly-reversed suspension of Kimmel over his Charlie Kirk comments.
