📈 Today's Deep Dive

MS NOW's $8 Streaming Bet Is Testing Whether Cable News Can Survive Its Own Funeral

Why It Matters
  • MS NOW's Sept. 9 "Membership" launch joins CNN's All Access and Fox One in a rush toward direct-to-consumer news streaming — an admission that the cable bundle that made these networks rich is no longer a sure thing.
  • Versant CEO Mark Lazarus is simultaneously diversifying into a golf tee-time platform and a $500M Full Swing simulator acquisition, suggesting he sees linear's revenue floor eroding faster than some executives, like Charter's Chris Winfrey, are publicly forecasting.
  • Niche, topic-focused streaming services grew 14% year-over-year in the U.S. — more than double the growth rate of premium SVODs like Netflix and Disney+ — giving MS NOW a real, if small, lane to compete in rather than a head-on fight it would lose.
The Money
  • MS NOW Membership runs $7.99/month ($79.99/year, $39.99 intro through Sept. 30) — cheaper than CNN's All Access and less than half of Fox One's $20/month.
  • The cautionary precedent is stark: CNN+ drew roughly 18,000 downloads in its first 24 hours in 2022 before David Zaslav shut it down a month later; Fox Nation's 2–2.5M subscriber figure hasn't been updated publicly since March 2025; Newsmax+ hasn't updated its 170,000-subscriber count since its 2023 launch.
Historical Context
  • CNN+'s 2022 collapse remains the industry's defining failure narrative, and every subsequent cable-news streaming launch — CNN All Access, Fox One, now MS NOW — is implicitly being measured against it.
Watch For
  • Whether MS NOW discloses real subscriber counts, unlike CNN's vague claim of the "largest jump in streaming subscribers on record."
  • Whether the reported sub-10% overlap between MS NOW's TV and digital audiences turns into genuine incremental revenue — or simply confirms the audience is too small to move the needle.
