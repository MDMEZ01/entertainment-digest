Paramount's Settlement Locks In $1.5B in Film Spend and an Editorial Watchdog for CNN/CBS

  • The 12-state deal requires $300M/year in added U.S. film production above 2025 levels and a rising annual slate, with penalties feeding worker funds if Paramount falls short.
    • $1.5B total over 5 years, plus a $25M fund for acquiring independent films; miss the 30-films (years 1-2) or 32-films (years 3-5) target and Paramount owes $30M per shortfall to union trusts, up to a forced Miramax sale.
    • A new "editorial independence" board will oversee CNN and Paramount-owned CBS News; Paramount avoided forced channel divestiture but must negotiate cable carriage separately for 5 years.
  • NY's Letitia James and CT's William Tong signed on too, ending speculation they'd hold out — James secured $17.5M for the WGA's health fund, Tong cited the CNN/CBS safeguards.
    • Also included: a $47.5M retraining fund for laid-off workers. The settlement still needs a judge's final sign-off before the merger can close.

📈 Today's Deep Dive

The Paramount Settlement Didn't Cost Ellison Structure — It Cost Him Time and Goodwill

Why It Matters
  • Bonta secured behavioral remedies — production spend, an oversight board — not the structural remedies (forced divestitures) he'd repeatedly said were the only acceptable outcome; critics like the Block the Merger coalition are already calling it a giveaway.
  • The new CNN/CBS "editorial independence" board is drawing skepticism from media watchdogs since it's appointed by the very company it's meant to police.
  • Timing wasn't incidental: resolving this before Oct. 1 matters because the deal's $7M-a-day ticking fee would otherwise have started accruing against Paramount.
The Money
  • $1.5B over 5 years in incremental U.S. film spend, plus $25M for indie acquisitions and a $47.5M worker-retraining fund — but missed film-count targets trigger $30M penalties per shortfall to union trusts, with a forced Miramax sale as the ultimate hammer.
  • The WGA's parallel suit settled for $17.5M into the guild's health fund plus a 5-year no-layoff pledge for CBS News writers.
  • Markets read the news asymmetrically: Paramount Skydance jumped roughly 4% Tuesday to $10.32, while Warner Bros. Discovery sat essentially flat at $30.80 — WBD had already rallied about 8% over the past month pricing in this exact outcome, so Tuesday's relief landed on the acquirer, not the target, per 24/7 Wall St.
Historical Context
  • It's the second time in as many years that a fight over CBS/CNN editorial independence has ended in a negotiated oversight board rather than a court fight — echoing commitments made when the original Skydance-Paramount merger cleared FCC review, which critics said went largely unenforced.
Watch For
  • Whether the settlement clears its last hurdle — a judge's sign-off — and whether the "force majeure" language Puck's Matt Stoller flagged (letting Paramount exit early on a recession or guild strike) ever gets tested before the deal's 2032 expiration.
  • How WBD-side staff cuts land once the deal formally closes; Bonta's press conference notably didn't address the job losses expected there.
