📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why a Fired FTC Commissioner Thinks He Can Kill the Paramount-WBD Merger

Why It Matters
  • Alvaro Bedoya, ousted from the FTC by Trump and now suing over his own firing, has spent weeks touring Hollywood rank-and-file workers in Atlanta, New York and Los Angeles gathering evidence for the case against Paramount-WBD.
  • His central argument to Puck's Bill Cohan: the 12 state AGs suing to block the deal have a "plain-vanilla," easily explainable case — that combining two owners of "a gazillion cable channels" will raise consumers' bills — and he believes it's a likely winner.
  • He's skeptical that Ellison's promised $6B in "synergies" can be achieved without mass layoffs among below-the-line workers, arguing no studio in modern history has ever produced the 30 theatrical releases a year the deal's backers have promised.
The Money
  • The merger carries $80B in combined debt that Ellison has pledged to pay down via the disputed synergies; analyst Rich Greenfield estimates the studios' current standalone plans total just 34 combined films in 2027.
  • PSKY pushed directly for a March 2027 trial rather than settling, which Bedoya reads as a sign the company's own lawyers had "some hard meetings with reality."
Historical Context
  • Bedoya draws on his own FTC track record — he says he's both won and lost merger challenges — and argues the AGs' six-separate-market theory is unusually strong compared to typical one- or two-market antitrust cases.
Watch For
  • Whether Wall Street banker Robert Wolf's warning proves out — that prolonged litigation uncertainty makes companies "more conservative," hurting the very jobs and competition critics claim to protect — as the March 2027 trial date approaches.
