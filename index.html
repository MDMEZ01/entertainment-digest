📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Disney's "Much Smaller Organization" Memo Is the Real Story of Hollywood's AI Reckoning

Why It Matters
  • The leaked internal memo — telling staff to stop "clinging to the [old] way" while executives are "moved, layered and pushed out" — is the clearest signal yet that a legacy media giant is restructuring around AI-driven efficiency rather than simply cutting costs in a down year.
  • The optics are stark: Disney legal chief Horacio Gutierrez warned his own division it would become "a much smaller organization... and some of you will personally be affected," even as the company has an open "Director of AI Enablement" role posted — the tool and the target of the cuts sitting side by side on the same careers page.
  • It lands the same week Disney's own dueling ex-CEOs, Bob Chapek and Bob Iger, are re-litigating who mismanaged succession and pay in public — a reminder that the memo isn't happening in a vacuum but atop years of unresolved leadership dysfunction Wall Street has never fully priced in.
The Money
  • Disney's cost-cutting push comes as rivals face their own AI-driven workforce math: Puck reports "more [cuts] are coming next week" at Disney, while the industry-wide fear — fewer workers doing more with AI tools — is now explicit enough that Puck's Krietzberg reports it has already spawned a multibillion-dollar AI-safety-and-infrastructure investment wave (Temporal Technologies at $12.55B, Factory at $5B) betting the disruption is structural, not cyclical.
Historical Context
  • It echoes 2021, when Disney (under Chapek) announced moving 2,000 Imagineering and parks jobs from Burbank to Florida before quietly canceling the plan under D'Amaro — a reminder that sweeping restructuring announcements at Disney don't always survive contact with reality, and that this memo's promises may prove similarly fluid.
Watch For
  • Whether the "next week" cuts Belloni is reporting land in the same divisions Gutierrez flagged, and whether Disney discloses any AI-attributable headcount reduction on its next earnings call.
  • Whether other legacy media companies (already destabilized by the Paramount-WBD settlement fight) issue similar memos — Puck's sourcing suggests David Ellison's California relocation threat already has executives elsewhere asking "did David just open Pandora's box?"
