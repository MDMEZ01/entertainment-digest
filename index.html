What I'm Hearing / Deadline

Ellison's California Threat Isn't About Texas — It's Leverage in a Bond Fight He's Already Winning

Why It Matters
  • Matthew Belloni argues the relocation "leaks" — a commissioned economic-impact report, a Politico story about Nashville real estate, TMZ's walked-back announcement claim — are pressure tactics aimed at AG Rob Bonta before the Oct. 1 ticking-fee deadline, not evidence of an actual move; Ellison has never publicly confirmed he's leaving.
  • Bonta isn't budging: his office calls the threat "blackmail" and says California's exit "lies at their feet," even as he racks up national press (Atlantic Festival, MS Now) that Belloni says the AG "covets."
    • Bonta made it official on the record Thursday, telling an Atlantic Festival panel in Manhattan: "They own it. It lies at their feet" — reiterating the threat isn't new and it's Ellison's call, not a response to his antitrust suit.
  • The federal track is now going Paramount's way regardless: the FCC cleared the merger's foreign-ownership structure Sept. 17 and the DOJ has formally backed Paramount's bond demand — leaving only the states' March 2027 antitrust trial as a real obstacle.
The Money
  • Ellison, RedBird Capital and Middle East backers face roughly $80B in debt once the WBD deal closes — before counting any ticking fees — making a Texas- or Nashville-style incentive package a real cost offset, not just a threat.
  • A $7.2M-per-day fee starts Oct. 1 if the bond dispute isn't resolved, with the next hearing expected around Sept. 24 and settlement talks penciled in for Oct. 14-15.
Historical Context
  • Larry Ellison's Oracle ran this exact playbook — California to Austin in 2020, then Nashville in 2024 for a reported $65M grant and up to $250M in tax breaks — yet Oracle has still struggled to recruit tech talent to Tennessee, a warning sign for a business, like Paramount's, that depends on concentrated creative talent.
Watch For
  • Whether Ellison ever files an actual relocation plan rather than leaking through intermediaries, and whether Govs. Gavin Newsom and incoming AG candidate Xavier Becerra can broker a settlement before the Oct. 1 deadline or the November midterms raise the political stakes further.
