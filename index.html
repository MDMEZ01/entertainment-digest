📈 Today's Deep Dive

The Real Winners of the Paramount-WBD Fight Weren't the Regulators — They Were the Arbs and the Bankers

Why It Matters
  • Merger arbitrageurs who bought WBD stock on July 20 — the day a judge's TRO paused the deal — and held through Monday's settlement captured roughly a 175% internal rate of return, per Puck's Bill Cohan, one of the best single trades of the year for anyone who read the legal risk correctly.
  • California AG Rob Bonta's reversal looks less like a negotiated win and more like a capitulation under pressure: Cohan reports heavy lobbying from Newsom, Karen Bass, Xavier Becerra and Silicon Valley donors frustrated by the delay, plus a weak underlying case — unremarkable concentration numbers, no opposition from rival studios or regulators abroad, and real appellate risk given a Supreme Court skeptical of broad antitrust theories.
  • The leverage that actually moved Bonta wasn't legal argument — it was Paramount's threat to force a $1.9B bond covering the deal's ticking fee, with a hearing that would have tested it set for the same week the settlement landed.
The Money
  • Banks are syndicating roughly $50B of the deal's debt this morning, pushing the combined company's total debt load to about $80B, while advisors on both sides split more than $400M in fees.
  • WBD's outgoing executives are walking away with outsized windfalls: CEO David Zaslav gets $550M+ in cash, CFO Gunnar Wiedenfels $120M, streaming chief JB Perrette $142M and strategy head Bruce Campbell $121M.
Historical Context
  • Had the deal collapsed instead of settling, Cohan estimates WBD stock could have fallen back toward the ~$7 level it traded at before Zaslav's 2025 split-up announcement — the gap between that and today's $31/share cash-out is the entire arb trade in one number.
Watch For
  • Today's 2pm ET hearing and the "Block the Merger" coalition's emergency motion — filed just this morning — are the last real chance to disrupt closing before the debt syndication and executive payouts become irreversible.
  • Whether Elon Musk's reported talks to join the investor syndicate change the optics of the "editorial independence" board Bonta touted as his win.
