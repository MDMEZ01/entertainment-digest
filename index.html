📈 Today's Deep Dive

The Federal Film Tax Credit Bill Is Real Now — And the Math Says the U.S. Just Became the Cheapest Place on Earth to Shoot

Why It Matters
  • Rep. Laura Friedman's bill was formally introduced today: a 20% credit on labor costs (including above-the-line pay, with VFX/post-production eligible if done domestically), requiring a $1M budget floor and at least 75% of production shot in the U.S.
  • Indie films can push the credit to 30% by shooting 30% of a project in economically distressed "opportunity zones" or across 10+ U.S. locations while spending $10M+; reality shows and existing series qualify, but talk shows, news, sports and commercials do not.
  • Unusual allies are already forming behind it — Rep. Adam Schiff has been "strategizing" with former reality star Spencer Pratt, per THR, a sign of how badly Hollywood wants this passed before Congress adjourns.
The Money
  • Stacked on top of state incentives, production-incentives VP Joe Chianese estimates a $100M shoot in California, Georgia, Illinois, New York, New Jersey or New Mexico would land at a combined effective rate of roughly 37-52% at the base 20% federal credit, rising to 43-58% at the full 30%.
  • That compares with roughly 18-30% in Canada, the U.K. and Australia before labor costs or FX rates are even factored in — "if it passes and it stacks clean, shooting in Georgia or California or New York is the best deal on the planet," Chianese said. "Nothing overseas comes close."
Historical Context
  • The push lands as overseas rivals brace to respond — France has already moved to strengthen its own rebate, and Chianese expects Canada and the U.K. to defend their production bases if the U.S. bill passes, meaning federal parity could touch off another round of overseas incentive hikes rather than end the race.
Watch For
  • Whether the bill clears committee before Congress adjourns this year — it would only apply to productions starting in 2027 if passed in this session.
  • Whether more states start facing relocation threats the way Paramount reportedly leveraged California during the WBD settlement fight — "did David just open Pandora's box?" one strategy exec asked Puck's Matt Belloni this week.
