📈 Today's Deep Dive

How Netflix Quietly Out-Lobbied All of Hollywood Combined — And What That Says About Where Power Is Headed

Why It Matters
  • Puck contributor Stephen Follows analyzed 27 years and 3,437 federal lobbying filings and found that in 2025, Netflix alone ($3.1M) out-spent the Motion Picture Association ($2.7M) — the first time a single studio out-lobbied the trade body representing the entire film industry, even though Netflix pays the MPA roughly $10M a year in dues.
  • The two camps want opposite things in Washington: Netflix's top lobbying subject is telecommunications and broadband policy (how its video reaches your TV), while the legacy-studio-led MPA has made copyright its top subject in 19 of the last 27 years — a structural divide between a distribution-first company and content-defending incumbents.
  • On AI specifically, studios logged roughly twice as many lobbying items as the guilds in 2025 and again in the first half of 2026 (32 to 14), showing the fight the 2023 strikes were supposed to settle has simply moved to Capitol Hill.
The Money
  • The MPA has spent $59.2M cumulatively on Washington lobbying since 1999; Netflix's first-half 2026 spend of $2.15M — partly during its failed bid for Warner Bros. Discovery's studio and streaming assets — already has it on pace to break its own 2025 record.
  • Netflix nearly doubled its retainer with Ballard Partners, the lobbying firm with deep Trump-administration ties (alumni include Susie Wiles and Pam Bondi), to $150,000 a quarter at the start of 2026; Paramount also retained the firm during the WBD bidding war.
Historical Context
  • Copyright's dominance in MPA filings traces back to the 2011-2012 SOPA/PIPA fight, one of the only times the tech industry has out-lobbied Hollywood outright — after which copyright's share of MPA lobbying dipped before recovering around "illicit streaming devices."
Watch For
  • AI-related lobbying items hit a record 23 per quarter in both quarters of 2026 so far; watch whether that keeps climbing as the No Fakes Act — which cleared the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously in June — moves toward a floor vote.
  • Second-half 2026 filings should show whether Netflix's spending accelerates further as the Paramount-WBD antitrust fight and CNN ownership questions intensify.
