📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Bonta v. Ellison Is Where the $111 Billion Paramount-WBD Merger Actually Gets Decided

Why It Matters
  • Bonta's direct personal attack on Ellison — "desperate," "grasping at straws," and earlier "blackmail" — signals no near-term settlement even as Cinema United pushes both sides toward one; the real fight is over cable-network concentration, an issue neither side has publicly resolved.
  • All three major theater chains (AMC, Regal, Cinemark) are now aligned behind Ellison's written exhibition guarantees for the first time, but exhibitor unity doesn't move the 12 state AGs, whose antitrust theory rests on network ownership, not theatrical windows.
  • SAG-AFTRA's refusal to back Cinema United's framework without "enforceable safeguards" on U.S. production shows labor isn't satisfied either — Ellison is fighting regulators and labor on two fronts at once.
  • Bonta's tone shifted Aug. 20 from "blackmail" to "coming to the table is always on the table" — the first real crack in the stalemate — though he still brands Paramount's posture as "playing cute" and insists on structural remedies, not just talk. New Today
The Money
  • Cinema United's four-point proposal (~30 wide releases/year, no rental-rate hikes, guaranteed catalogue access, anti-bundling safeguards) would lock in billions in guaranteed theatrical inventory for exhibitors if adopted.
  • Paramount's threat to relocate out of California by Oct. 1, plus its earlier $1.9B bond maneuver, raise the financial stakes of continued regulatory delay for the combined company.
  • A new LA County study pegs the merger's local cost at roughly 10,000 jobs (4,500 production, 2,500 executive) and $79M in lost tax revenue over three years — fresh ammunition for both labor and the state AGs. New Today
Historical Context
  • The standoff echoes DOJ v. AT&T–Time Warner (2018), another vertical media-merger antitrust fight where courts ultimately sided with the deal parties over the government's theory — a precedent Ellison's camp may be counting on if the case reaches its March 2027 trial.
Watch For
  • Whether Bonta and Ellison actually sit down now that both sides have used the language of dealmaking, even as the substance remains unresolved.
  • Whether SAG-AFTRA's holdout becomes the next flashpoint now that exhibitors have fallen in line behind Ellison.
  • The Sept. 24 hearing where a judge rules on Paramount's $1.9B bond request, landing a week before its ticking-fee penalty period begins. New Today
