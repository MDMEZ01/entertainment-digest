📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Why a March 2027 Trial Date Is Actually the Warner-Paramount Deal's Best-Case Scenario

Why It Matters
  • The judge's 12-day trial window, set to begin March 2, 2027, splits the difference between Paramount's push for a fast November date and the 12 state AGs' and WGA's request for a slower April track — a compromise that removes scheduling uncertainty even as it extends the limbo.
  • A firm date lets both sides plan: Paramount can now model its ticking-fee exposure with precision, and WBD can set standalone contingency plans against a known trial window rather than an open-ended fight.
  • The compromise also tells state AGs and the WGA the court is taking their antitrust and labor arguments seriously enough to reject Paramount's fast-track request outright.
The Money
  • Paramount's ticking fee to WBD shareholders begins October 1, 2026 at roughly $7 million per day, a bill that could top $1.5 billion by the time a verdict arrives given the trial doesn't even start until March.
  • The deal cannot close until five days after trial concludes or June 1, 2027 — whichever comes first — effectively locking in close to a year of merger limbo from today.
Historical Context
  • The dynamic echoes the 2017-18 AT&T-Time Warner antitrust fight, where a drawn-out trial delayed but ultimately didn't kill the merger — though this case carries added political weight given the ongoing dispute over CNN's editorial independence.
Watch For
  • Whether Paramount tries to peel off holdout state AGs with settlement terms before March to avoid trial altogether.
  • Any standalone contingency-planning commentary from WBD executives as the company continues to report quarterly results throughout the delay.
  • Industry commentary — including an Ankler op-ed floating George Clooney as a hypothetical buyer — is already using the deal to illustrate how few players beyond the Ellisons could absorb WBD's price tag.
