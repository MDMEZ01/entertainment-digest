Bonta's Cancelled Meeting Shows the Paramount-WBD Settlement Talks Were Never as Close as the Spin Suggested

Why It Matters
  • California's AG walking away from a scheduled mediation session — and publicly accusing Paramount of leaking and misrepresenting Friday's closed-door talks — signals the two sides are further apart than the "settlement is close" narrative pushed in recent days.
  • The move also exposes a widening political split: Newsom and LA Mayor Karen Bass have leaned toward a deal, while Bonta, mayoral challenger Nithya Raman, and gubernatorial candidate Xavier Becerra are positioning against one — turning a corporate antitrust case into a live wedge issue in California's 2026 political calendar.
  • LA County's own economic report putting the merger's local damage at $2.87B gives Bonta fresh public-interest cover to hold the line rather than cut a quick deal.
  • The breakdown coincides with Mark Ruffalo's public feud with Paramount, which branded his Oracle-Israel criticism "antisemitic" — a charge he called "appalling and fundamentally dishonest" on Saturday, adding another combustible front to the deal's PR war.
The Money
  • Paramount's standing offer — roughly 30 films a year and 45-day theatrical exclusivity — has repeatedly been called structurally toothless by Bonta's office, meaning the studio may need to sweeten terms materially to restart talks after this breach of trust.
Historical Context
  • It echoes the AT&T–Time Warner fight a decade earlier, where a studio combination survived antitrust scrutiny only after a bruising, headline-driven legal battle — a precedent both sides are clearly studying as they posture publicly rather than negotiate quietly.
Watch For
  • Whether Paramount responds with a public statement disputing Bonta's "bad faith" characterization, and whether Newsom's office intervenes again to try to force both sides back to the table before goodwill erodes further.
  • Any sign the March 2027 trial date gets pulled forward or pushed back as a result of this breakdown.
