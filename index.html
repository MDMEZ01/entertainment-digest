📈 Today's Deep Dive

Anthropic's IPO Goes Global, and Its Governance Becomes the Story

Why it matters
  • A Nov. 2 filing shows Anthropic will sell up to ¥552.9B (about $3.5B) of shares to Japanese investors as part of its U.S. listing, a sign the deal is being built for a global book.
  • Puck's Bill Cohan reports that seven co-founders, through a single Class F share held by Founder LLC, control 50.1% of the vote on most stockholder matters, and that this control is not diluted by new share issuance.
  • Governance expert Nell Minow called it the "most bananas corporate governance structure in history" and warned that quick index inclusion leaves passive investors little choice but to own it.
The money
  • The S-1 shows first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3B, up from about $1.16B a year earlier, with an $80.7B net loss of which $72.4B is non-cash fair-value changes.
  • Puck says the raise could reach $100B at a $1.5T–$2T valuation, with up to $750M in fees for lead underwriters Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley; Reuters says some investors have discussed about $1.5T.
Historical context
  • Alibaba's September 2014 NYSE debut raised about $25B and was then the largest IPO ever, ahead of Agricultural Bank of China's $22.1B; Anthropic's expected raise would be about four times that and above SpaceX's $75B June IPO, per Reuters.
Watch for
  • Investor marketing could begin the week of Nov. 9 (Motley Fool), though no date has been announced.
  • Whether the Long-Term Benefit Trust fills its vacant trustee seat and fourth board seat before pricing; Reed Hastings, Vas Narasimhan and Christopher Liddell hold the other three.
