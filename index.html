Netflix vs. YouTube: The Attention War Netflix Can't Win on YouTube's Terms

Why it matters
  • YouTube took 15.8% of U.S. TV viewing in August versus Netflix's 7.7%, down from Netflix's 9% peak last December, per Nielsen's Gauge.
  • Puck's Julia Alexander argues creator deals like Druski's can't make Netflix the default: about 80% of YouTube users go there when they can't find something elsewhere (Hub).
  • Omdia says music videos are about a third of YouTube viewing, news about 10% and podcasts about 5% — categories Netflix barely touches.
The money
  • Alphabet said YouTube ad revenue grew just 9% to $11.2B last quarter, with over a third coming from TVs, while YouTube Premium and Music top 150M subscribers.
  • Netflix's moat is retention: Antenna puts its churn at about 2% versus roughly 6% for peers.
Historical context
  • Netflix told shareholders in January 2019 that it competes with "and loses to" Fortnite more than HBO — the same attention-war framing, now aimed at YouTube.
Watch for
  • Druski's SNL hosting turn, which Puck bills as a first for a digital creator, as a test of creator-to-mainstream crossover.
  • Next Gauge release: Enders found U.K. viewers added about 13 YouTube minutes a day over two years while Netflix stayed flat.
