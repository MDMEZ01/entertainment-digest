📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Netflix Cuts 1,000 Jobs as Hollywood’s Layoff Wave Reaches the Winner

Why it matters
  • Netflix began cutting about 1,000 jobs, roughly 6% of staff, on Oct. 14 — its largest reduction since 2022 and bigger than the ~850 Puck reported last week.
  • Cuts fall mostly on product/technology, legal and HR, a sign the industry’s healthiest streamer is now trimming overhead rather than content.
  • Rushfield argues the business has adopted a private-equity playbook: “acquire, consolidate, slash costs, extract value, repeat.”
The money
  • Shares were about $70.32 before the news, down ~20% this year and more than 40% over 12 months, with Morgan Stanley and Barclays cutting targets to $80 and $70.
  • Q3 earnings land Oct. 20 after the close, with consensus at $0.82 EPS on $12.88B in revenue.
Historical context
  • In 2022, after reporting its first subscriber loss in a decade, Netflix cut about 150 jobs in May and roughly 300 more in June — today’s round is more than double that combined.
Watch for
  • Whether the Oct. 20 letter backs management’s pledge to hit FY26 targets, as Apptopia shows lapsed U.S. users up just over 16% year over year in Q3, a seventh straight rise.
  • Whether Skydance, now carrying the WBD debt load, follows with cuts of its own.
