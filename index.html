📈 Today's Deep Dive

Inside the Federal Film Tax Credit Bill Trump Just Endorsed — and the Six Things That Could Sink It

  • Why It Matters
    • For the first time in over 30 years, politicians, studios, guilds and even the Teamsters are rowing in the same direction: Trump's endorsement lets Republicans openly back the Motion Picture, Television, and Entertainment Revitalization Act, which could be introduced in the House as soon as the week of September 14.
    • Rep. Laura Friedman (D-CA), a former '90s film producer, is expected to co-lead with Georgia Republican Brian Jack, whose district includes the former Marvel-hosting Trilith Studios — a genuinely bipartisan pairing in an otherwise polarized Congress.
    • The Directors Guild reportedly signed a side letter during its last studio negotiation requiring leadership to support a federal incentive, and DGA president Chris Nolan may speak out publicly if needed.
  • The Money
    • The baseline pitch is a 20% credit on labor costs, stackable on top of state incentives — to meaningfully compete with the U.K. and Canada, the program likely needs to throw off $4–5 billion a year, matching what individual states already spend combined.
    • Puck's Belloni argues the credit should cover "above-the-line" costs like Tom Cruise's and Margot Robbie's salaries too, since the U.S. currently subsidizes foreign incentive programs anyway through the foreign tax credit mechanism when stars film abroad.
  • Historical Context
    • Echoes how Canada's federal credit interacts with provincial ones: Canada's 16% base rate "grinds" down to roughly 6% of a budget once provincial credits are netted out — exactly the design flaw U.S. drafters are being warned to avoid.
  • Watch For
    • Whether the bill stays an "overlay" on existing state film offices rather than creating a new national film office — which insiders fear could open the door to politically selective project approval.
    • Few expect a floor vote before the November 3 midterms, but MPA chief Charlie Rivkin is set to release an economic-impact report on the incentive during the same mid-September week the bill could be introduced.
