📈 Today's Deep Dive

Ellison's $6.6B Sports Bill: Skydance Owns the Most Rights in America — and May Start Selling

Why it matters
  • After merging Paramount with Warner Bros. Discovery, Skydance holds at least 36 sports rights deals, the most of any U.S. company, from the NFL and March Madness to NASCAR, the PGA Tour and UFC.
  • The Ankler's Lesley Goldberg reports Skydance may be weighing exits from Big Ten football, NASCAR and the French Open, even as it pushes buyouts ahead of layoffs.
  • A person familiar with its thinking cautions "the ink on the merger deal is still drying," and Skydance declined to comment.
The money
  • Industry estimates put the rights bill north of $6.6B a year against about $80B of debt.
  • The three candidates for sale cost a combined $515M a year: Big Ten ($350M), NASCAR ($100M) and the French Open ($65M).
Historical context
  • A rival executive points to Turner, which lost its NBA rights after the 2024–25 season: "Without the NFL, it's a tougher situation; look at what happened when Turner lost the NBA."
Watch for
  • The NFL, whose 2021 deals run $110B through 2033, wants richer terms before its 2027 season and is expected to use an opt-out with most partners, including CBS, after 2029.
  • Whether Skydance cuts rights or cable networks first, with more than 50 networks now under Ellison's control.
