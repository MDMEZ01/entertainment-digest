📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Does Zaslav Need a Plan B? Wall Street's Growing Doubts on the Warner Deal

Why It Matters
  • WBD trading nearly 20% below the $31.75/share PSKY would owe if the deal drags to June 2027 is the market's clearest signal yet that closing is no longer a sure thing.
  • The March 2, 2027 Oakland trial date and the $7M-a-day ticking fee starting October squeeze Paramount's balance sheet regardless of how the antitrust case turns out.
  • Ellison's New York Times op-ed reframing the fight as political — paired with Ari Emanuel's own pro-merger op-ed days earlier — reads increasingly like a coordinated campaign rather than independent voices rallying to the deal.
The Money
  • June 4, 2027 is the date David Zaslav can walk away and collect a $7B breakup fee from Larry Ellison, the personal guarantor of PSKY's capital structure.
  • Wolfe Research's Peter Supino has floated an alternative endgame — Paramount keeps HBO, sells Warner Bros. to Netflix, and offloads the cable networks to private equity — a scenario he argues could be a windfall for arbs regardless of how the main deal resolves.
Historical Context
  • Echoes the 2017-18 AT&T-Time Warner fight, where a drawn-out trial delayed but didn't kill the merger — though the breakup-fee structure here gives Zaslav a cleaner off-ramp than Randall Stephenson ever had.
Watch For
  • Whether WBD's share price keeps drifting further from the $31.75 takeout price as the March trial date approaches — a real-time referendum on deal-closing odds.
  • Whether Zaslav's team quietly lines up Plan B buyers before the June 2027 walk-away date forces his hand.
