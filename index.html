📈 Today's Deep Dive

Versant's $7.99 MS NOW "Membership" Is a Referendum on Whether Cable News Has Any Streaming Future

Why It Matters
  • Versant CEO Mark Lazarus is launching an $8-a-month direct-to-consumer tier for MS NOW (formerly MSNBC) — cheaper than CNN's new All Access and far below Fox One's $20 — betting there's a durable niche lane even though executives concede they can't compete with Netflix or Disney+ for broad audience dollars.
  • Puck's Julia Alexander frames it as a test case for the whole cable-news category: MS NOW still draws 1–1.3M nightly primetime viewers, but that bundle-era economics — profiting even when few are watching — no longer exists post-cord-cutting.
  • The bet leans on real data: niche, topic-focused "specialty SVOD" services grew 14% year-over-year in the U.S., more than double premium SVOD's growth rate, suggesting some appetite for narrowly targeted streaming exists even if news itself is a small slice of it.
The Money
  • MS NOW's own research found under 10% overlap between its TV and digital audiences — a number that could mean real incremental subscription revenue, or could simply mean the two audiences are too small and disconnected to sustain both businesses.
Historical Context
  • The precedent is grim: CNN+ drew only about 18,000 downloads in its first 24 hours in 2022 before WBD's David Zaslav killed it within a month, and Fox Nation (2–2.5M subs as of last update) and Newsmax+ (170,000 subs at launch) have both gone dark on public numbers since.
Watch For
  • Whether MS NOW Membership publishes real subscriber figures — unlike its rivals — as an early signal of whether this niche-news bet is working.
  • Whether Versant's move pushes CNN or Fox to disclose harder numbers on All Access and Fox One rather than the vague superlatives used so far.
