📈 Today's Deep Dive

The Real Fight Over Warner Bros. Discovery Isn't Netflix vs. Paramount Anymore — It's Paramount vs. 12 State AGs

  • Why It Matters
    • Paramount Skydance already beat Netflix for WBD in February and cleared shareholder, DOJ and EU review by summer — the deal itself is decided; what isn't is whether a 12-state antitrust coalition, led by California's Rob Bonta, can still reshape or kill it before a March 2027 trial.
    • Paramount's new motion — asking the court to force the states and the WGA to post a $1.9 billion bond — would test how much financial risk a challenger must absorb just to bring an antitrust case, a precedent regulators elsewhere are watching closely.
  • The Money
    • A $0.25-per-share "ticking fee" to WBD shareholders starts accruing Oct. 1 at roughly $7 million a day, and could top $1 billion in accumulated cost by the time the trial opens in March 2027.
    • Paramount's bond motion asks the states and WGA to personally cover that ticking-fee exposure if Paramount ultimately prevails at trial.
  • Historical Context
    • Bond-and-delay fights like this are rare in merger litigation; they echo the financial brinkmanship of the long-running AT&T-Time Warner case, where delay itself became a bargaining chip.
  • Watch For
    • Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin's ruling on the bond request, which will signal how the court views the states' odds heading into the March 2027 trial.
    • Whether the growing ticking-fee bill pushes Paramount toward a settlement with the states before trial.

  • The Figure
    • Paramount asked the court to require the 12 suing states and the WGA to post a $1.88-$1.9 billion bond covering its costs while their antitrust case delays the $110B WBD merger.

  • So What
    • The WBD saga has shifted from a Netflix-vs-Paramount bidding war (Paramount already won in February) to a Paramount-vs-states cost fight that could reshape merger litigation strategy well beyond this one deal.
