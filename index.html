📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Why Ellison Is Betting a Trial Buys Time, Not Just a Verdict

Why It Matters
  • Per Puck's William D. Cohan and Dylan Byers, David Ellison has walked away from settlement talks with the state AGs entirely, opting to fight the Paramount-WBD antitrust case at trial — a bet that has less to do with winning the case than with buying time to decide whether the deal still makes sense.
  • Because the merger's drop-dead date isn't until June 4, 2027, a trial — win or lose — keeps the option open for the Ellisons and David Zaslav to reconsider the deal as market conditions evolve.
  • The standoff between Gov. Newsom's office (reportedly urging a settlement) and AG Bonta (publicly ruling one out) shows the politics inside California's own government are as unsettled as the deal itself.
  • In a fresh Aug. 2 Puck interview, Bill Cohan put the strategy even more bluntly: the Ellisons "realized that they were wrong about the regulatory fight and that they probably overpaid for WBD" — reframing the trial gambit as a face-saving offramp as much as a legal strategy. New Today
The Money
  • Walking away entirely would cost $7 billion to WBD plus a $2.8 billion breakup fee to Netflix (from Netflix's own prior aborted bid) — $9.8 billion total, versus closing a $113 billion combined deal that requires $80 billion in fresh debt and puts $42.5 billion of equity at risk.
  • Zaslav's own economics center on $31.75 a share (the original $31 offer plus $0.25/quarter in ticking fees); closing gets him a personal payout north of $550 million, while Paramount Skydance alone carries $12 billion in net debt against a market cap of roughly $8 billion.
  • Cohan puts that $8 billion figure in perspective: Netflix is worth $300 billion and Disney $167 billion, and even a combined PSKY-WBD at $113 billion would still trail every other major player in Hollywood. New Today
Historical Context
  • The standoff echoes AT&T's grinding 2018 fight to close its Time Warner acquisition, where a drawn-out antitrust trial eroded market confidence even after the deal ultimately closed — a reminder that the wait itself can cost nearly as much as the fight.
  • Cohan calls Zaslav's own process a "textbook" auction: the Ellisons' initial $19-a-share bid was rejected, Zaslav put WBD into Revlon-mode play, and competition from Netflix's aborted bid ultimately pushed the price to $31 a share in cash — a 63% jump — with the largest breakup fee ever attached and Larry Ellison personally backstopping the equity and debt. New Today
Watch For
  • Whether the court grants Paramount's requested November trial date or sides with the states' push to delay until April.
  • Continued WBD talent poaching by Ellison's camp as executives hedge against a deal collapse, per Lesley Goldberg's reporting that anyone without face time with Ellison is "100 percent taking those meetings" elsewhere.
