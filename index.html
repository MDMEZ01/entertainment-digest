📈 Today's Deep Dive

Wall Street Is Now Financing the AI Buildout — and Chipmakers Are Backstopping It

Why it matters
  • Broadcom signed a $63B financing in which a special-purpose vehicle buys Broadcom chips and leases them to Anthropic, with BofA, Citi and Morgan Stanley arranging senior debt and Blackstone taking the riskier tranche.
  • Broadcom backs the chips' residual value, giving lenders protection on about $43.5B of the senior debt, per Reuters, so the supplier is effectively underwriting its own customer.
  • Anthropic's S-1 shows why: $542B–$567B in compute commitments, including up to $120.5B for SpaceX compute through 2029.
The money
  • Meta priced $25B of common at $625 plus about $12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred, with Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom as anchor buyers, while a SpaceX filing confirms a binding ~$40B Apollo-backed GPU lease financing.
  • Hock Tan: "This validates that there is capital available to finance such growth."
Historical context
  • Telecom gear makers such as Lucent and Nortel lent billions to carriers to buy their equipment in the late 1990s, then wrote much of it off when those customers failed in 2001–02.
Watch for
  • Capex guidance from Microsoft and Alphabet on Oct. 28 and Amazon on Oct. 29.
  • Anthropic's roadshow, which Bloomberg says could start the week of Nov. 9 for a pre-Thanksgiving debut.
