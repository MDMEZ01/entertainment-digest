📈 Today's Deep Dive

Netflix Beat on Profit, Then Lost Wall Street on Growth

Why it matters
  • Netflix posted $12.87B revenue, up 11.8%, and $0.87 EPS against $0.82 expected, yet shares fell about 6% after hours to a 52-week low just under $65.
  • It cut its 2026 ad-revenue target to about $2.7B from $3B, and Greg Peters admitted "we are currently a bit behind our aggressive target."
  • Ted Sarandos said "we do need better execution in the U.S." days after ousting Bela Bajaria and Dan Lin and cutting about 1,000 jobs.
The money
  • Q4 guidance of $13.44B revenue (+11.5%) and $0.67 EPS trailed the Street's roughly $0.73, with layoff costs shaving 1.2 points off a 26% margin.
  • The Wakeup notes the ad tier still earns less per U.S. subscriber than ad-free plans, and the CFO signaled more price increases in 2027.
Historical context
  • The last comparable reset came in April 2022, when Netflix's first subscriber loss in a decade sent the stock down about 35% in a day and led to layoffs that spring.
Watch for
  • Peter Rice's first moves as CCO and a Netflix "channels" offering for rival streamers, which Peters hinted is coming.
  • 2027 guidance arrives in January; Needham already downgraded to Hold and Barclays says the stock "may not recover through this year."
