Netflix's Reset: Peter Rice Inherits a Valuation Problem, Not Just a Hit Problem

Why it matters
  • Peter Rice takes over Netflix's content operation Monday, Oct. 26, after Bela Bajaria and film chief Dan Lin were pushed out.
  • Puck's Matt Belloni reports co-CEOs Greg Peters and Ted Sarandos also laid off about 1,000 employees in the purge.
  • The Entertainment Strategy Guy argues Netflix's core weakness is an "IP problem" that lowers its hit rate, which a new executive won't fix.
The money
  • ESG notes Netflix's P/E reached about 50 last summer, versus a 10-to-20 historical range (average 16) for mature entertainment companies.
  • His take: the eight-month stock swoon means Wall Street "recovered its senses," so the house-cleaning may have been a mistake.
Historical context
  • After its 2022 stock crash, Netflix pivoted to advertising, a password-sharing crackdown and sports, per ESG.
Watch for
  • ESG flags possible next pivots: weekly vs. binge releases, more theatrical, linear channels and bundles, and creator or vertical video.
  • Belloni says the successors inherit the same structural issues, including theatrical resistance and the limits of the binge model.
