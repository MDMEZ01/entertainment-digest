📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Ellison May Have to Sell Off Cable Networks to Save the WBD Deal

Why It Matters
  • To clear the last hurdle in his $110B+ Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition — a suit from a dozen state attorneys general led by California's Rob Bonta — David Ellison may need to offer "structural remedies," meaning divesting some of the roughly 50 cable networks a combined Paramount-WBD would control.
  • Paramount pushed back hard in an official statement today, insisting settlement discussions remain confined to "the AG's office and authorized Paramount leadership" and calling other accounts "speculation, not fact" — a sign the company is trying to control the narrative as pressure mounts.
  • The Ankler's Lesley Goldberg surveyed more than half a dozen cable executives; none believe Ellison would ever give up CNN, suggesting any divestiture would target smaller or overlapping brands instead.
The Money
  • Brands under scrutiny include Comedy Central, Cartoon Network and Adult Swim — insiders float combining them into a single comedy hub rather than selling outright — while other networks are described as "worth surprisingly little" on their own.
  • The maneuvering echoes recent precedent: NBCUniversal spun USA, MSNBC, CNBC, Golf Channel, Syfy, Oxygen and E! into Versant, while Disney sold its 50% stake in ten A+E channels to Hearst for roughly $1B.
Historical Context
  • It mirrors cable's broader retreat from the 2010s peak-TV land grab, when scale in linear networks was the goal; today, dealmakers increasingly treat cable brands as bargaining chips to clear antitrust hurdles for streaming-and-studio combinations.
Watch For
  • Whether talks between Paramount and Bonta's office resume after this week's reported breakdown, with a $7M-a-day fee attached to the broader settlement structure once October arrives.
  • Which of the "two buyer categories" and the unnamed "wild card with a history of disruptive offers" Goldberg alludes to actually emerges for any divested networks.
