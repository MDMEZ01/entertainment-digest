📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Paramount Blinked: Why Agreeing to a 2027 Trial Is a Quiet Defeat for David Ellison

Why It Matters
  • Paramount stipulated it won’t close the $111B WBD deal until five days after an antitrust trial or June 1, 2027 — whichever comes first — effectively conceding it can’t muscle the merger through this year.
  • The 12-state coalition (led by California) got what a TRO alone couldn’t guarantee: a full trial on the merits, handing regulators leverage they rarely win before a deal even closes.
  • With the WGA withdrawing its own injunction motion, the fight narrows to the states — but the timeline, not the merits, may end up deciding the outcome.
The Money
  • The contract’s ticking clock bites hardest here: WBD shareholders are owed roughly $650M per quarter — about $6.9M per day — if the deal isn’t closed by Sept. 30, turning delay into a direct, compounding cost.
  • A trial as late as April 2027 (the states’ proposal) means three-plus quarters of that carry, pressuring Ellison to settle, restructure or walk.
Historical Context
  • Antitrust challengers rarely need to win outright; as with prior blocked media megadeals, the delay itself — not a final ruling — is often what kills or reshapes the transaction.
Watch For
  • July 31: the parties file a joint stipulation on trial scheduling — the first read on whether this drags to April 2027 or moves faster.
  • Any sign Ellison seeks divestitures or a revised structure to shorten the runway before the Sept. 30 penalty wall.
