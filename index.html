📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Why the Paramount-WBD Opposition Actually Worked This Time

Why It Matters
  • The Ankler's Richard Rushfield argues the deal's unraveling isn't really about antitrust doctrine — it's that people stopped believing consolidation was inevitable once early dissenters like Jane Fonda and Mark Ruffalo, and the Future Film Coalition's blockthemerger.com, made opposing Ellison look possible rather than futile.
  • Monopoly expert Matt Stoller now puts the odds of the deal being blocked at 60%, a dramatic shift from earlier this year when Hollywood treated the merger as inevitable, much as it did with Disney-Fox — a comparable deal that drew zero organized resistance.
  • Bonta escalated the rhetoric this week, calling Paramount's threatened California exit "blackmail," while CA gubernatorial nominee Xavier Becerra publicly urged both sides to settle — signs the political temperature is rising across the board.
The Money
  • Bonta says he's open to "structural remedies" — keeping certain corporate entities separate in movies and cable TV — but has rejected PSKY's behavioral offer of a written 30-films-over-3-years pledge to theater chains as insufficient.
  • PSKY's Makan Delrahim countered that "everything is on the table" while arguing "there is no such thing as basic cable anymore" — a notable claim given cable supplied 73% of PSKY's Q2 EBITDA.
Historical Context
  • Rushfield contrasts this fight with Disney-Fox, the last deal of comparable scale, which also closed under a Trump administration to the benefit of a Trump-friendly mogul — yet drew no meaningful opposition at all.
Watch For
  • Whether DGA and IATSE's new nine-point framework (separate studios, protected theatrical windows, HBO staying linear) becomes the template Rushfield says no one had yet floated — see today's Breaking News.
  • Whether Paramount's implied October relocation threat pushes Bonta to the table or hardens the 12-state coalition further as more Democrats like Becerra lean publicly toward settlement.
