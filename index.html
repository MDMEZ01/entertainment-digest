The Wakeup / THR

Paramount's California Exodus Threat Just Got a Paper Trail — and a Motive That Has Little to Do With Texas

Why It Matters
  • PSKY told L.A. Mayor Karen Bass and California AG Rob Bonta this week that it intends to leave the state, following a month-old public-records disclosure that CEO David Ellison met Texas Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick about relocation incentives.
  • The threat surfaces roughly two weeks before a ticking-fee clock starts on PSKY's antitrust bond fight over the WBD merger — a court fight that continues no matter where the company is headquartered.
  • Austin and Nashville have both reportedly been scouted and apparently stalled, and TMZ had to walk back a report that PSKY would announce a move this week — suggesting the threat may currently be more leverage than concrete plan.
The Money
  • A relocation is, bluntly, a savings play: PSKY needs cash for its highly leveraged buyout, and Texas or Tennessee incentives — tax credits, training grants, property-tax abatements — could offset real costs against a roughly $1.9B bond exposure.
Historical Context
  • Larry Ellison's Oracle ran this exact playbook: it left California for Austin in 2020, then moved again to Nashville in 2024 for a reported $65M grant and up to $250M in tax breaks — though Oracle has also struggled to recruit tech talent to Tennessee.
  • A combined WarnerParamount would likely keep at least two studio backlots in L.A. even in a relocation scenario; moving a physical-production business is far harder than moving a software company's HQ.
Watch For
  • Whether PSKY actually files a relocation announcement, after this week's on-again, off-again reporting.
  • The bond-fight hearing expected around Sept. 24, which will shape how much cash pressure PSKY is really under regardless of its address (see ICYMI).
