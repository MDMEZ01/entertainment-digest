📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Hollywood's IP Land Grab Is Now Playing Out in Britain

Why It Matters
  • At the Edinburgh TV Festival, Belloni convened three top U.K. producers (Banijay's Patrick Holland, Nutopia's Jane Root, Raw's Dimitri Doganis) who laid out how U.K. law forces the BBC and Channel 4 to grant producers I.P. ownership — while Netflix and Amazon largely don't, converting the U.K. into a work-for-hire market just like the U.S. did a decade ago.
  • As BBC funding erodes and cord-cutting mirrors the U.S. experience, streamers are "filling the void" for commissions without conferring the ownership rights that built the U.K.'s unscripted export business (MasterChef, Traitors).
  • The same week, Microsoft's Xbox pitch to sell its game catalog wholesale to a studio shows the identical dynamic playing out in a different medium: whoever controls the underlying I.P. increasingly dictates the economics.
The Money
  • Doganis says Netflix typically won't pay more than $1.2M-$1.5M for a true-crime documentary, even one — like "The Tinder Swindler" — he says has been watched by "half a billion people," with producers retaining none of the upside.
  • Root's Nutopia is testing an alternative: launching shows directly on YouTube with stars like Will Smith and Trevor Noah so the production company and talent retain ownership before any secondary sale to a streamer.
Historical Context
  • Holland argues Banijay's just-closed All3Media merger gives it library scale to negotiate rather than simply supply — echoing how U.S. studios used consolidation to claw back leverage from streamers after years of buyout-driven talent and I.P. flight.
Watch For
  • The BBC's royal charter renewal in 2027 and whether U.K. and E.U. regulators impose "forced prominence" rules requiring platforms to surface public-broadcaster content.
  • Whether Banijay actually "takes the gun out of the holster," in Holland's words, and withholds library titles from streamers to force better economics.
  • Whether Xbox's parallel push to license its game catalog wholesale reshapes ownership norms for game-to-screen adaptations the way streaming reshaped U.K. television.
