📈 Today's Deep Dive

Why Ellison's "Ninth-Inning" Settlement Talks Are Really a Game of Chicken With California

Why It Matters
  • Ellison's self-imposed Oct. 1 relocation deadline is squeezing CA AG Rob Bonta to settle before a $7M-a-day fee starts accruing on the stalled $110B Paramount-WBD deal, per Puck's Matt Belloni.
  • Bonta's public line — "They own it. It lies at their feet." — signals Sacramento isn't spooked yet, even as Texas officials and Nashville boosters actively court Paramount's headquarters.
  • THR reports Bonta's possible leverage points include forcing WBD and Paramount to operate independently short-term, outside content-licensing commitments, a jump to 30 combined films a year (from 3), and independent oversight of CBS News and CNN — any of which would reshape the merged company for years.
The Money
  • Ellison, RedBird Capital and Middle East backers are on the hook for roughly $80B in debt if the deal closes, with a $1.9B bond hearing next week that Paramount wants states and the WGA to help front.
  • The FCC already cleared about $24B in foreign financing (Saudi PIF, Qatar Investment Authority, Abu Dhabi Investment Authority), handing foreign investors a 49.5% stake in the combined company and raising the cost of leaving the antitrust piece unresolved.
Historical Context
  • THR draws a parallel to 1934, when studio chiefs threatened to flee California for Florida over a gubernatorial candidate — a threat that proved to be a bluff, suggesting Ellison's relocation talk may be leverage more than an actual plan.
Watch For
  • Whether a settlement lands before Ellison's Oct. 1 deadline, and whether its terms include the independent-oversight conditions floated for CBS News and CNN.
  • Any confirmation of Nashville as the "first choice" alternate HQ if talks stall — real estate scouting there has already been reported.
