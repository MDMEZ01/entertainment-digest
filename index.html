📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Why Paramount's $111 Billion Gamble Now Hinges on Outlasting the Clock, Not Winning the Case

Why It Matters
  • California AG Rob Bonta told The Ankler point-blank that the states challenging the Paramount–WBD merger won't negotiate: “We're not going to have oligarchs or kings in the economy” — a signal this fight is heading to trial, not a settlement table.
  • Monopoly expert Matt Stoller, who correctly predicted the states' intervention months in advance, now puts Paramount's odds of losing the deal at 60%, whether through a courtroom defeat or the financing simply collapsing under the strain of delay.
  • Puck's Kim Masters reports the Ellison camp's decision to freeze next steps reflects genuine alarm about how exposed the deal has become — and WBD executives, sensing the wobble, are increasingly taking meetings with rivals rather than waiting it out.
The Money
  • MoffettNathanson's Robert Fishman argues time itself, not the eventual verdict, is Paramount's biggest enemy: the real question for investors is no longer just whether the merger closes, but whether Paramount can financially survive the wait.
  • Paramount's legal team is pushing for a November trial date rather than the states' requested April delay, calling that ask a “stonewalling tactic” — itself a tell that further delay is viewed internally as the greater risk.
Historical Context
  • The standoff echoes AT&T's grinding 2018 fight to close its Time Warner acquisition, where a drawn-out antitrust trial eroded market confidence even after the deal ultimately closed — a cautionary precedent for how the wait itself can cost nearly as much as the fight.
Watch For
  • Whether the court grants Paramount's November trial date or sides with the states' push to delay until April.
  • Continued poaching at WBD as talent hedges against a deal collapse — per Lesley Goldberg, anyone who hasn't yet met with David Ellison is “100 percent taking those meetings” elsewhere.
