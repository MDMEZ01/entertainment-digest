📈 Today's Deep Dive

Skydance's $11B Stock Drop Is a Debt Story, Not a Warrants Story

Why it matters
  • Skydance shares fell 24% on Oct. 14, to $7.06 from $9.27, about a week after the Ellisons closed their $110 billion Warner Bros. Discovery deal.
  • The trigger was about 471 million warrants, valued at $1 billion with a $12 strike and 10-year life, issued to holders of the roughly 9% of shares outside the Ellison syndicate, yet the company lost about $11 billion in value.
  • Puck's Bill Cohan attributes the "extra $10 billion hit" to $80 billion of debt, a 7x debt-to-EBITDA ratio and ratings downgrades, while Skydance's view is that automated selling made the market overreact.
The money
  • Cohan writes that Bank of America, lead underwriter on $12 billion of junk bonds, "kind of blew the pricing," and the new-issue debt has traded down.
  • Minority holders' stock plus warrants was worth $9.84 a share Friday, up 6.1% since Tuesday, while the Ellison syndicate's stake has lost about $12 billion, nearly 30%, since the deal closed.
Historical context
  • Warner Bros. Discovery itself launched in April 2022 carrying roughly $55 billion of debt from the Discovery–WarnerMedia merger, and paying it down defined the company's next three years and its share-price slide.
Watch for
  • Debt markets expect Larry Ellison to inject equity so leverage can fall to 3x by 2029; an Ellison insider says a "clear plan" needs no new equity, and Cohan points to the recent Fitch report.
  • Skydance's first earnings call in February 2027, which The Wakeup says will include Warner Bros.' "Digger," headed to about $18 million domestic on a $160 million-plus production spend.
