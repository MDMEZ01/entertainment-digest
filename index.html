📈 Today's Deep Dive

The Capex Reckoning: Wall Street Starts Punishing the AI Build-Out

Why it matters
  • Alphabet fell 12.6% on Thursday, its worst day since its 2004 IPO, erasing about $482B as AI spending pushed cash flow negative.
  • Hours later Amazon sank more than 6% after hours even as AWS grew 39% to $45.98B, its fastest pace in nearly five years.
  • Investors are no longer rewarding cloud growth alone; they are pricing the cash it takes to get there.
The money
  • Amazon spent $75.9B on capex in the quarter, keeps about $220B for 2026, and Andy Jassy expects "meaningfully more" in 2027.
  • Amazon's trailing-12-month free cash flow swung to -$42.4B from +$14.8B a year earlier.
Historical context
  • The closest parallel is Meta's February 2022 plunge of roughly 26%, which erased about $230B as investors balked at metaverse spending — and Meta answered with a "year of efficiency."
Watch for
  • Apple reports fiscal Q4 on Monday, Nov. 2 — the one megacap without a giant AI capex bill.
  • Anthropic's planned Nasdaq IPO will test how much investors will pay for the model makers that consume this spending.
