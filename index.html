📈 Today’s Deep Dive

Ellison's CNN Confession: The Paramount-Warner Fight Was Never Really About Antitrust

Why It Matters
  • In his first public remarks since the states' suit was filed, Ellison told NYT readers the real question is "whether I can be trusted as a steward of Warner's CNN" — not market concentration, undercutting his own legal team's antitrust-only defense strategy.
  • Hours later, Ari Emanuel's CNBC pitch for a CNN/CBS News "editorial board" implicitly conceded that trust, not competition law, is the real battleground — an unusual admission for a company mid-litigation.
  • The reframing hands the 12 state AGs (and Trump, who has repeatedly cited CNN in his own commentary on the deal) a political narrative just as both sides head toward a spring trial.
The Money
  • Paramount is barred from closing the $111 billion deal until five days after trial or June 1, 2027 — whichever comes first — while WBD shareholders collect a 25-cents-per-quarter "ticking fee" (roughly $7M/day) for every quarter the deal stays open past Sept. 30, 2026.
  • The WSJ reports Paramount Skydance has held early talks with former Activision CEO Bobby Kotick and Meta president Dina Powell McCormick about seats on a prospective "Warnamount" board.
Historical Context
  • The suit, led by California AG Rob Bonta and 11 other Democratic state AGs, argues the merger would concentrate too much power in cable and theatrical markets — the economic argument Ellison's op-ed notably doesn't engage with directly.
Watch For
  • Whether Ellison's political framing helps or hurts Paramount's position ahead of the spring trial date state AGs requested.
  • Whether Emanuel's "editorial board" concept becomes a formal settlement offer rather than a talking point.
