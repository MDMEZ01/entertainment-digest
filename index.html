📈 Today's Deep Dive

From Cash to Credit: Big Tech Starts Borrowing to Build AI

Why it matters
  • Amazon disclosed a supplier loan facility of up to $80B secured by the AI hardware it buys, available from early 2027 through Q3 2028.
  • Its free cash outflow hit $42.4B over the 12 months to September, while total spending commitments of $883B more than doubled in a year.
  • Debt is spreading across the sector: Alphabet issued $28.7B of senior notes after a second straight quarter of negative free cash flow (−$9.1B).
The money
  • Amazon plans $220B of 2026 capex even as AWS grew 39% to $46B, its fastest pace in nearly five years.
  • Blackstone now expects Anthropic to carry a double-B "high junk" rating after its IPO, not investment grade before 2027, per the FT.
Historical context
  • The late-1990s telecom fiber boom was also financed largely with debt and vendor credit, and it ended in the 2001–02 collapses of WorldCom and Global Crossing when demand lagged capacity.
Watch for
  • Whether Microsoft and Meta follow with asset-backed facilities, and how credit markets price Anthropic's IPO roadshow, reportedly starting the week of Nov. 9.
  • AWS CEO Matt Garman says Amazon is "looking for ways to also responsibly fund that."
